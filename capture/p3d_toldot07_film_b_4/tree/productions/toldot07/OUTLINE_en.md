## 1. LESSON THESIS (3 sentences)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The Great Sea (Yam HaGadol, the Mediterranean, also called Yam HaTichon) is the western border of Eretz Yisrael, fixed by the Torah ("Gevul Yam"), and it is a border of water without fence or gate; the sea itself keeps the limit set for it - the wind raises waves, but soft sand stops them (Yirmeyahu 5:22, "asher samti chol gevul layam").
2. The sand of our beaches came from the Nile, the waves carried it up to Haifa Bay and the wind piled it into dunes and kurkar rock; because our coast is almost straight with almost no bays, ships need a man-made quiet place - a port behind a breakwater - and the ports (Yafo in the past, Haifa and Ashdod today) are the "Sha'ar HaAretz", the gate of the land, through which nearly all goods arrive.
3. Far from shore the sea is deep, dark and cold, full of life (fish, dolphins, sea turtles) and treasures (drinking water, salt, gas), which answers the lesson question "What does the Mediterranean hide, when from its shore we see only the surface?"

Other framing items of the lesson:
- Lesson question (question slide): "What does the Mediterranean hide, that we see from it only the surface of the water?"
- Reveal text under the question: "By the end of the lesson we will know: why it has two names, who raises the waves, where the sand came from, why walls of rocks are built in the sea, and what hides in the dark 1,700 meters under the ships."
- Key sentence (printed in the workbook, read aloud at the end): "Waves, sands and ports - what does the Mediterranean hide in its depths?"
- Key sentence answer (teacher's answer): "The Great Sea is the western border of the land: waves raised by the wind, sand that came from the Nile, ports that are the gate of the land, and depths full of life and treasures."
- Hook scenario (from content hook): evening at the sea near Ashdod, about 35 km in a straight line from Beit Shemesh; wave after wave breaks on the sand and withdraws; far away a loaded ship sails; the sun slowly goes down and disappears at the line where sea touches sky; from here to the horizon only water. The class is asked to stand and point toward where they think the sea is, and check against film 1.
- Structure of the lesson: opener + hook, question, agenda of five parts (each part = one film of about 4 minutes + a quiz), surprise "Yam!" after part A, a Rabbi Akiva "did you know" after part B, a writing task after part C, the Zevulun "did you know" after part C, the gold coins surprise after part D, a records slide, a glossary slide, the final writing task (t4), the summary film (film_z), the key sentence slide; optional: jellyfish "did you know", two riddles, ending slide (next lesson: the Kinneret = "HaKinneret"), credits.
- Order of slides in the plan: title; a0_opener; a0_hook; question; agenda; section A; film_a; a9_quiz; s1_surprise; "Yam echad, shemot rabim" slide; Rashi fish fact; section B; film_b; b9_quiz; Yirmeyahu quote slide; Rabbi Akiva slide; section C; film_c; c9_quiz; sand-in-hand activity; t1_task; check of task 1; Zevulun slide; section D; film_d; d9_quiz; s2_surprise; class question "what here came by ship"; section E; film_e; e9_quiz; Tehillim 107 quote slide; records slide; glossary slide; t4_task; film_z; key sentence slide; optional jellyfish, riddles, ending, credits.
